2. PRIMITIVE COEFFICIENT AND SIGN CONVENTION

3. LARGE-PRIME SUPPORT
LargePrimes proves integrality of the actual monomial pencil for p>N using
the integral quotient block and actual reflected low-pair cancellation.
The basis change multiplies its determinant by the actual factorial prefactor.

4. RESTRICTION RANK AND LOCAL SCALING
RestrictionPencil uses actual coefficients of the central basis after local
substitution (c+pY)^2, for actual maximal reflected classes c and coefficient
indices 0,...,D. Here D=floor((50n-2)/p), so the restriction row cardinality is
(D+1)*w_p. No row dimension is replaced by a guessed rank.
The actual pencil is multiplied by p^(5+epsilon), epsilon=floor(2(N mod p)/p).
The local functional/radical theorem supplies coefficientwise integrality and
one additional p-factor for every vector in the restriction kernel.
RestrictionDeterminant uses a residue-field kernel basis, integral rational
lifts and a unit determinant to gain r-min(r,(D+1)*w_p).
Unscaling therefore gives precisely
  v_p(F)>=-(4+epsilon)*24n-min(24n,(D+1)*w_p).
The hypotheses are p>5 and p^2>100n+6. These cover all degree bounds in the
actual quotient/Bernoulli/derivative proof; no abstract radical premise remains.
The maximal-class formula and epsilon are identified with the actual real
rank profile. The half-integer class-count difference contributes at most
(floor(50n/p)+1)/2, hence at most 641 when p/(20n)>=1/512.
Since epsilon<=1 and the minimum is <=r, the uniform charge is <=6r=144n.

6. ALL PRIMES AND CUTOFFS
SmallPrimeEntries proves the actual all-prime coefficient bound with cost
24n*(9*Nat.log p(100n+4)+v_p(96)); no odd-prime restriction is used there.
SmallPrimeSum sums it using elementary log bounds over primes no larger than
Y=100*(floor(sqrt(n))+1). This gives the explicitly defined smallPrimeError.
If p>Y, then p>5 and p^2>100n+6. For n>=100000000, Y<floor(20n/512).
The exact intervals 0<p<=Y, Y<p<=floor(20n/512), and
floor(20n/512)<p<=50n partition the retained prime charge without overlap.
The middle initial interval is bounded by 144n*theta(floor(20n/512)); theta
at that integer equals theta(20n/512). Negative actual valuations only improve
this upper bound. SmallPrimeError/(20n)^2 tends to zero.
